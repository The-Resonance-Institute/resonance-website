2. The pattern

Three kinds of correction recur, and the report names them as findings about method rather than as apologies.

  1. A claim stated as an absolute when the evidence supported a scoped version. “Impossible”, “regardless of how actions are framed”, “the world’s first”, “the corner is empty”. Each was narrowed to the claim the evidence carries. The narrow claims survived; none of the absolutes did.
  2. A metric whose label was the system’s own output. The injection figure is the clearest case: “catastrophic” had been defined as “what the gate refused”. The general rule now applied to every number: who defined the positive class, and could they have defined it before seeing the system’s answer.
  3. A figure carried in context after the record moved. The test count in the builder’s instructions, the live-surfaces account, the advisor’s numbers. The remedy is the ledger: a figure is re-derived or traced on the day it is used, or it is not used.

The change of thesis is a different thing and is reported as one. Between June (“the conscience is in the architecture, not in the agent”) and October (“it has to live inside the model”), what moved was not a figure but the conclusion the figures support. Section 11 of the report is about that.
